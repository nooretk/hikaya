## 1. Short Overview

The Old City of Jerusalem is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the
world. It sits at the heart of Jerusalem and covers roughly 0.9 square kilometres,
yet it contains a very large number of religious and historical sites. It is
surrounded by stone walls built in the 16th century CE under the Ottoman Sultan
Suleiman the Magnificent, with eight main gates. The Old City is divided into four
quarters — Muslim, Christian, Armenian and Jewish — and is a spiritual and cultural
centre for millions of people worldwide.

## 2. Key Historical Timeline

| Period | What happened |
|---|---|
| Canaanite era (from the Early Bronze Age, 4,000+ years ago) | The Canaanites are believed to have founded the city, known as Urusalim / Urshalem. The name is thought to derive from the Canaanite deity "Shalem". They built defences and water systems; the Gihon Spring was the main water source. |
| Ancient Israelite era (c. 1000 BCE) | According to accounts in the Old Testament, Jerusalem became the capital under King David, and his son Solomon built the First Temple. |
| 586 BCE | The Babylonian king Nebuchadnezzar II attacked the city, destroyed the First Temple, and deported a large part of the population (the Babylonian Exile). |
| Persian era (from 539 BCE) | Cyrus the Great took Babylon and permitted the return to Jerusalem and the rebuilding of the Temple; the Second Temple was built around 516 BCE. |
| Hellenistic era (from 332 BCE) | Alexander the Great conquered the region; control passed between the Ptolemies and the Seleucids. Attempts to impose Greek culture led to the Maccabean revolt in the 2nd century BCE and a period of Hasmonean independence. |
| Roman era (from 63 BCE) | Pompey entered Jerusalem. Under Herod the Great the city saw major construction and the Second Temple was expanded. In 70 CE, after the First Jewish Revolt, Titus besieged the city and destroyed the Second Temple; part of the retaining wall survives and is known as al-Buraq Wall (the Western Wall). |
| Byzantine era (324–638 CE) | After Constantine I adopted Christianity, Jerusalem became a major Christian holy city. The Church of the Holy Sepulchre was built in 335 CE. Churches, monasteries, roads and markets expanded. |
| Islamic conquest (638 CE) | Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab entered the city peacefully at the request of Patriarch Sophronius, without destruction or displacement. He issued the *Umariyya Covenant* guaranteeing the safety of the inhabitants, their property and their places of worship. |
| Umayyad era (661–750 CE) | The Dome of the Rock was built by order of Caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, completed in 691–692 CE. Caliph al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik completed Al-Aqsa Mosque in the early 8th century CE. |
| Abbasid and Fatimid eras | Repeated restoration work after earthquakes, including rebuilding parts of Al-Aqsa Mosque after the major earthquake of 1033 CE. Schools, libraries and pilgrim facilities were built. |
| Crusader occupation (1099–1187 CE) | The First Crusade took the city after a long siege. Historical sources record widespread violence against Muslim and Jewish inhabitants. Jerusalem became the capital of the Latin Kingdom. |
| Ottoman era (16th century CE) | Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent rebuilt the city walls that still stand today. |
| 1981 / 1982 | The Old City and its walls were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (1981) and added to the List of World Heritage in Danger (1982). |

## 3. Cultural and Religious Importance

- **Islam:** Al-Aqsa Mosque is the largest and most important site in the Old City for
  Muslims. "Al-Aqsa" refers to the entire walled compound, which includes the Qibli
  Mosque, the Dome of the Rock, the Marwani Prayer Hall, the Dome of the Chain, and
  dozens of domes, porticoes, wells, minarets and historic schools.
- **Christianity:** The Church of the Holy Sepulchre, built in the 4th century CE under
  Emperor Constantine, is one of the most important churches in the world. Several
  Christian denominations share its administration under a historic arrangement known
  as the *Status Quo*.
- **Judaism:** Al-Buraq Wall (called the Western Wall by Jews) is one of the most
  important places of prayer in Judaism, as part of the retaining wall of the compound
  that surrounded the Second Temple. A common custom is placing small written prayers
  between its stones.
- **Living heritage:** traditional crafts passed down through Jerusalemite families —
  Palestinian embroidery (*tatreez*), olive-wood carving, hand-engraved copperware, and
  blue-and-white decorated ceramics used in mosques, churches and homes.
- **Food heritage:** musakhan, maqluba, qidra khalili, Nabulsi kunafa, and ma'moul
  prepared for feasts and holidays.
- **Customs:** welcoming guests with Arabic coffee, exchanging dishes between neighbours
  during Ramadan and holidays, decorating markets with lanterns in Ramadan, and
  religious celebrations held in the historic squares.

## 4. Main Landmarks

- **Al-Aqsa Mosque compound** — includes the Qibli Mosque, the Marwani Prayer Hall and
  the Dome of the Chain.
- **Dome of the Rock** — completed 691–692 CE; one of the oldest Islamic buildings still
  standing, known for its golden dome, mosaics, Qur'anic inscriptions and octagonal plan.
- **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** — built in the 4th century CE.
- **Al-Buraq Wall (Western Wall)** — part of the Herodian-era retaining wall.
- **City gates** — eight historic gates, the best known being Damascus Gate (Bab al-Amud)
  and Jaffa Gate (Bab al-Khalil); others include Bab al-Sahira, Bab al-Asbat,
  Bab al-Maghariba, Bab al-Nabi Dawud, the New Gate, and the sealed Golden Gate.
- **Traditional markets** — Khan al-Zeit, Souq al-Qattanin, Souq al-Attarin,
  Souq al-Lahhamin.

## 5. Important Facts

- Area: approximately 0.9 km².
- Surrounded by Ottoman walls roughly 4 km long.
- Contains more than 220 historical monuments.
- Four main quarters: Muslim, Christian, Armenian and Jewish.
- Eight historic gates.
- UNESCO World Heritage List: 1981. List of World Heritage in Danger: 1982.
- Architecture uses Jerusalem limestone, narrow stone-paved alleys, stone arches, domes,
  inner courtyards and decorated windows, showing Canaanite, Roman, Byzantine, Islamic,
  Mamluk and Ottoman influences.

## 6. Religious Traditions (NOT to be presented as historical fact)

- **Al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj** — according to Islamic belief, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was taken
  by night from the Sacred Mosque to Al-Aqsa Mosque and then ascended through the
  heavens. This is why Jerusalem holds great spiritual standing for Muslims.
- The name **al-Buraq Wall** is linked in Islamic tradition to the Prophet tethering the
  Buraq there on the night journey.
- Christian tradition identifies the Church of the Holy Sepulchre as containing the site
  of the crucifixion, the tomb, and the resurrection.

## 7. Oral Heritage and Popular Stories (NOT historical fact)

- **The Holy Fire ceremony** — held annually at the Church of the Holy Sepulchre during
  Orthodox Easter; described in the source as one of the oldest Christian rites in
  Jerusalem.
- **Market stories** — merchants pass down accounts of the historic markets receiving
  caravans from Damascus, Cairo and the Hijaz, exchanging goods and cultures.
